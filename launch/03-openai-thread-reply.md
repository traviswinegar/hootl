# OpenAI Thread Reply

The Blueprint announcement is at:
https://x.com/OpenAINewsroom/status/2062225755854274629

Three options below — single tweet for the cleanest land, 3-tweet
thread if you want the substance to survive without a clickthrough,
quote-tweet if you'd rather post from your own timeline and let the
quote pull the Blueprint in.

**Rules of thumb for posting in OpenAI's thread:**
- Reply within a few hours of the original. The thread loses
  algorithmic life after ~24h; reply timing matters more than reply
  quality after the first day.
- Don't @ OpenAI. The reply is already on their thread. @-ing reads
  needy and gets you nothing.
- Don't lead with criticism. Frame additively. The Blueprint is a
  serious document; the gap is structural, not a failure.
- Lead with the *substantive claim* (operator-side layer), not the
  meta-claim ("here's my framework"). Frameworks-in-a-vacuum get
  ignored. Substantive claims with a citation get followed.
- One link per tweet. hootl.org for option A; hootl.org for tweet 1
  + the launch piece for tweet 3 in option B.

> What it doesn't address — and structurally can't — is the substrate
> properties of the autonomous-agent *runtime* that wraps a deployed
> model. Eight principles for that operator-side layer:
> hootl.org

**Character count:** ~280 (X's limit is 280 for unverified, 4000 for
Premium — fits either way).

**Why this works:**
- Names the Blueprint's scope honestly ("the lab-side safety story")
  rather than mischaracterizing it
- States the substantive gap clearly ("operator-side runtime")
- Doesn't claim the Blueprint is wrong — claims it's incomplete by
  design
- Links to the framework, not to the launch piece (lower-friction
  destination)
- "Eight principles" gives the reader a reason to click — concrete,
  bounded, scannable

  skeptic has a third-party reference
- Tweet 3 lands the framework with two links — one for the principles
  (low friction), one for the full argument (for the engaged reader)

## Recommendation

**Go with Option A as the reply in OpenAI's thread + Option C as a
top-level post on your own timeline.** Costs nothing extra and
doubles the surface area:
- The reply gets you the search-indexed discovery later

Skip Option B unless you have a specific reason to expect engagement
on a thread (you usually don't, in someone else's reply tree).

## When to post

**Same day the launch piece publishes.** The Blueprint announcement
is 24 hours old as of this writing; the algorithmic life is fading
but the discovery window is still real. The pieces should land in
this order:

1. Standalone piece goes live on Substack/Medium
2. Same hour: top-level QT of the Blueprint announcement from your
   timeline (Option C), with hootl.org link
3. 15 minutes later: reply in the OpenAI thread (Option A)
4. Same day: send 2-3 of the outreach emails (start with METR + one
   journalist — the two highest-leverage ones)

Don't post the reply before the launch piece is up — a clickthrough
to hootl.org with no contextual write-up loses the argument. Don't
wait more than 6 hours after the piece either — the Blueprint thread
will be cold.

## What NOT to do

- Don't post a reply consisting of "🧵 here are eight principles..."
  with no link or framing. Read as a self-promo bot, ignored.
- Don't reply with just the hootl.org link and nothing else. Looks
  like spam.
- Don't claim the Blueprint is wrong, dangerous, or anti-safety. It
  isn't, and you'll lose every serious reader in the first sentence.
- Don't tag Sam Altman or any OpenAI exec. Doesn't help; reads
  thirsty.
- Don't get drawn into thread arguments. Reply once, link, move on.
  If someone substantive engages, take it to DM or email.
- Don't apologize for the framework being "still in development" or
  "open to feedback." Land it like it's the framework, because it is
  the framework. Stake = credibility.
